# The parent summary

Write it after the child has left, in the parent's language, with the child's words quoted in the original. Aim for about one screen: honest, specific, and kind. Don't grade, compare the child with others or diagnose. Describe what happened and what to try next. Lead with the step the child took on the new rung today: the summary is about helping them climb, not about measuring them.

## Template

```
<Book> · <date> · mode A/B · about <n> minutes · main floor: <n> · new rung: <rung>

In short (3–5 sentences)
The step the child took on the new rung today (and how much help it took), what else they did well,
and where to pick up next time.

Ladder record
<rung> <help 0–3 today> · <rung> <help> · …
Secure (unaided in 2 of the last 3 talks): <rungs>  ·  Next new rung: <rung>

Question by question
1. <question, short> — "<child's words, short quote>" — help: 0/1/2/3 — what it shows
2. …

Their reading tips (in their own words)
- "<…>"

Words they didn't know
<word> (how it came up) · …

Connections they made
<to self / another book or film / the world>

Next time (1–2 small steps)
- the new rung for next time (or the same one, if it isn't secure yet), and which strength to start the bridge from
- e.g. "Still on 1b. She went straight to feelings again, so ask for three sentences without the scaffold and see if she starts with who wants what."
- e.g. "When they say 'it's more interesting', offer two or three nearby words and let them choose."

Ask again in about 4 weeks (word for word, to see change)
- "<question>"
- "<question>"
```

## What to watch for across talks

- **The ladder:** which rungs are secure, which are moving (help levels dropping), and which have stalled. A rung stuck at help 2–3 for three talks needs a smaller step or a different bridge, not more of the same.
- **Help levels on types 5–6** (words and the author's choices, floors 2–3) dropping over time. This is the slowest skill, and it holds only once floor 1 does.
- **The author as the one choosing:** the child says "Montgomery doesn't tell us…" without being asked about the author. Note the exact words and whether you had named the author first.
- **Stance:** held with a reason / changed with a reason / changed without one.
- **Types that keep getting skipped:** check the last few summaries. If summing up (type 2) or any other type was skipped every time, plan a real question for it next time. A talk that goes well on one thing can quietly crowd the rest out for weeks.
- **The school's target:** if the school has set a reading target, say in each summary what the child did towards it today.
- **Gist gates:** if they're frequent, the books may be too hard. Suggest mode B, or a slightly easier book.
